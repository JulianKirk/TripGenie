# Activity accessibility guide

General guidance for planning activities for travellers with disability,
reduced mobility, or other access needs. TripGenie records three accessibility
facts for each activity: wheelchair accessible, step-free access, and
accessible toilet. Each fact can be confirmed yes, confirmed no, or unknown.

## Activity accessibility: confirmed versus unknown accessibility

Accessibility information is shown as yes, no, or unknown. Yes means the
feature has been confirmed. No means it has been confirmed as unavailable.
Unknown means nobody has confirmed it either way, and it must not be read as
accessible. When an access need is essential and the value is unknown,
contact the operator before booking. Accessibility filters only match
activities where the feature is confirmed yes.

## Activity accessibility: wheelchair access and step-free access

Wheelchair accessible means a wheelchair user can enter and take part in the
main activity. Step-free access means the route from the entrance to the
activity has no steps, using ramps, lifts, or level paths; it also helps
travellers with prams, walking frames, or luggage. An activity can have
step-free access without being fully wheelchair accessible, for example when
paths are level but too narrow or the activity itself is physical.

## Activity accessibility: accessible toilets and facilities

Check for an accessible toilet at or near the activity, especially for
half-day or longer activities and boat or coach trips. Ask whether there is
seating, shade, and a quiet space for breaks. Read any accessibility notes on
the activity for details such as hearing loops, large-print material, sensory
sessions, mobility-aid hire, or assistance-animal policies.

## Activity accessibility: outdoor, water, and adventure activities

Outdoor, water, and adventure activities vary widely in access. Many national
parks have short sealed or boardwalk paths suitable for wheelchairs, while
bushwalking tracks are often steep and uneven. Boat trips may have steps or
gangways at boarding. Snorkelling, kayaking, and climbing can sometimes be
adapted with notice. Ask the operator about boarding, toilets on board, and
whether staff can assist.

## Activity accessibility: companion cards and concessions

In Australia, people with disability who need a support person may hold a
Companion Card, which many participating venues accept so that the companion
attends free or at reduced cost. Many attractions also offer concession prices
for seniors, students, and pension card holders. These arrangements are set by
each venue, so check with the operator before booking; TripGenie's listed
price does not include concessions.

## Activity accessibility: planning tips for access needs

Contact the operator ahead with specific questions rather than general ones,
for example the width of doorways or the number of steps at boarding. Allow
extra time between activities for transfers. Prefer quieter times of day,
which are usually easier for travellers with sensory needs. Keep a backup
activity nearby in case an access arrangement falls through on the day.
