# Activity booking and pricing guide

General guidance on booking activities and understanding activity prices.
TripGenie lists activity prices in Australian dollars (AUD). TripGenie does not
sell tickets, take payments, or hold reservations; when an activity needs a
booking, the traveller arranges it directly with the operator.

## Activity pricing: per-person and flat-admission prices

Activities are priced in one of two ways. A per-person price is charged for
every participant, so the total cost is the listed price multiplied by the
number of people. A flat-admission price is one charge that covers the whole
admitted party, so the total cost stays the same whatever the party size,
within the activity's participant limits. For example, a per-person tour
listed at 50.00 AUD costs 200.00 AUD for four people, while a flat-admission
private experience listed at 200.00 AUD also costs 200.00 AUD for four people.

## Activity pricing: comparing prices and estimating totals

When comparing activities, compare the total for the whole party rather than
the listed price alone. A price filter compares the listed price, not the party
total, so a cheap-looking per-person activity can cost more than a
flat-admission one for a large group. A free activity has a listed price of
0.00 AUD. Remember to budget separately for transport to the venue, food,
equipment hire, and optional extras such as photos, which are often not
included in the listed price.

## Activity booking: when a booking is required

Some activities can be joined on the day, while others must be booked in
advance with the operator. Booking is usually needed for guided tours,
boat trips, tastings, classes, timed-entry attractions, and anything with a
small maximum group size. If an activity says a booking is required, arrange
it before adding fixed times to the itinerary. Read the activity's booking
notes for guidance such as how far ahead to book.

## Activity booking: how far ahead to book

Book popular and small-group experiences early. As a rule of thumb, book at
least a day ahead for small-group tours and tastings, a week or more ahead for
reef, island, and multi-day trips, and several weeks ahead during school
holidays, public holidays, and major events. Walk-in attractions such as
museums, galleries, parks, and markets rarely need advance booking, but timed
exhibitions sometimes do.

## Activity booking: cancellations, refunds, and weather

Check the operator's cancellation terms before paying. Many operators offer a
full refund when a booking is cancelled 24 to 48 hours ahead, and little or no
refund for later cancellations or no-shows. Weather-dependent activities, such
as boat trips, kayaking, and reef tours, may be cancelled by the operator for
safety; in that case operators usually offer a transfer to another day or a
refund. Keep booking confirmations and the operator's contact details handy.

## Activity booking: minimum numbers and group bookings

Some activities only run when a minimum number of participants have booked.
If too few people book, the operator may cancel or move the session, so
confirm a day before. Group bookings above the published maximum usually need
to be split across sessions or arranged as a private booking, which may be
priced as a flat charge instead of per person.
