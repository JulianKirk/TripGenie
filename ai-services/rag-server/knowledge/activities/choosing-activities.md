# Choosing activities and attractions

General guidance for choosing activities and attractions that suit the
traveller, the party, and the trip. TripGenie classifies every activity with
one or more categories: Adventure, Culture, Family, Food and drink, Nightlife,
Outdoor, Shopping, Tour, Wellness, and Wildlife.

## Choosing activities: matching activities to travellers

Start with what the party enjoys and how much energy it has, then narrow by
place, date, and budget. Active travellers often combine Adventure and Outdoor
activities such as hikes, kayaking, or snorkelling. Travellers who prefer a
slower pace usually enjoy Culture, Tour, Food and drink, and Wellness
activities. Mixed groups work well with one shared main activity per day and
optional extras that people can join or skip.

## Choosing activities: what each activity category means

Adventure activities are high-energy and may carry physical requirements.
Culture covers museums, galleries, and heritage sites. Family activities suit
children and usually have low minimum ages. Food and drink covers tastings,
food walks, and cooking experiences. Nightlife covers evening entertainment.
Outdoor activities happen mainly outside and depend on the weather. Shopping
covers markets and local makers. Tour covers guided or self-guided tours.
Wellness covers relaxation, yoga, and fitness. Wildlife covers animal
encounters and nature observation.

## Choosing activities: attractions versus guided experiences

An attraction such as a museum, landmark, or park is often visited at the
traveller's own pace for a single admission charge. A guided experience at the
same place, such as a curator-led museum tour, is a separate activity with its
own price, duration, group size, and schedule. Choose the guided version when
context and commentary matter, and the self-paced visit when flexibility and
cost matter more.

## Choosing activities: activity duration and time of day

Check the expected duration and add travel time to and from the venue. Short
activities of one to two hours combine well with a second activity on the same
day. Half-day and full-day activities, such as reef trips or winery tours,
usually fill most of the day. Sunrise activities such as kayaking or yoga suit
early risers and avoid the midday heat. Sunset cruises and food crawls suit
evenings. Plan physically demanding activities for the morning, when energy is
highest and temperatures are lower.

## Choosing activities: rainy-day and weather alternatives

Outdoor, Wildlife, and many Adventure activities depend on the weather and
operators may cancel or reschedule them for wind, storms, heat, or poor sea
conditions. Keep at least one indoor alternative in mind for each outdoor day,
such as a museum, gallery, market hall, or food tour. Schedule weather-sensitive
activities early in a stay so there is room to move them to another day.

## Choosing activities: activities for children and families

Check the minimum age, and any maximum age, before choosing an activity for a
family. Prefer activities of two hours or less for young children and include
breaks, food, and toilets nearby. Family, Wildlife, and many Outdoor
activities, such as gentle walks, wildlife parks, and interactive museums,
suit children well. Teenagers often enjoy Adventure activities once they meet
the operator's age and height requirements.

## Choosing activities: activities for groups

Check both the minimum and maximum number of participants. Some activities run
only when enough people book, and small-group tours have a strict upper limit,
so a large group may need to split across sessions. For groups, compare the
total cost rather than the listed price: per-person activities scale with the
group size, while flat-admission activities charge one price for the party.
