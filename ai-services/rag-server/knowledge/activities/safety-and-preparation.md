# Activity safety and preparation guide

General guidance on preparing for activities in Australia and staying safe.
Always follow the operator's safety briefing and instructions, which take
priority over general advice.

## Activity preparation: what to bring for outdoor activities

For outdoor activities such as walks, hikes, and wildlife tours, bring water,
a hat, sunglasses, sunscreen, comfortable closed shoes, and a light layer for
changing weather. Carry snacks for activities longer than two hours. Insect
repellent is useful near rainforest, wetlands, and at dusk. Charge your phone
before setting out and check whether the area has mobile coverage.

## Activity preparation: what to bring for water activities

For kayaking, snorkelling, boat trips, and reef tours, bring swimwear, a towel,
reef-safe sunscreen, a hat with a strap, and a change of clothes. Use a dry bag
for phones and valuables. Operators normally supply life jackets, snorkels, and
masks, and on tropical reef trips they often supply stinger suits. Take
seasickness tablets before a boat trip if you are prone to motion sickness,
and tell the operator about any swimming limitations.

## Activity safety: sun and heat in Australia

The Australian sun is strong all year, and UV levels are often high even on
cloudy days. Use broad-spectrum sunscreen of SPF 30 or higher and reapply
every two hours and after swimming; wear a hat, sunglasses, and covering
clothing; and seek shade in the middle of the day. In hot weather, drink water
regularly, plan strenuous activity for the early morning or late afternoon,
and rest if you feel dizzy, nauseous, or develop a headache.

## Activity safety: swimming and beaches

Swim at patrolled beaches between the red and yellow flags, which mark the
area watched by lifesavers. Rip currents are common on Australian surf beaches;
if caught in one, stay calm, float, and signal for help rather than swimming
against it. In tropical northern Australia, dangerous jellyfish known as
marine stingers are most common roughly from November to May; swim in stinger
nets where provided and wear a stinger suit on reef trips.

## Activity safety: wildlife encounters

Watch wild animals from a distance and never feed them. In northern Australia,
saltwater crocodiles live in rivers, estuaries, and coastal waters, so obey
warning signs, do not swim where crocodiles may be present, and stay well back
from the water's edge. On wildlife cruises and tours, stay seated when asked
and keep hands inside the boat. Snakes generally avoid people; if you see one,
keep still and then move away slowly.

## Activity safety: bushwalking and hiking

Choose walks that match the fitness of the slowest person in the group. Check
park alerts, track closures, and fire danger ratings before setting out, and
avoid bushwalking on days of extreme or catastrophic fire danger. Tell someone
your route and expected return time, stay on marked tracks, and start early
enough to finish in daylight. Mountain areas such as the Blue Mountains can be
much cooler than the nearby coast, so pack a warm layer.

## Activity safety: age, health, and fitness requirements

Respect the operator's minimum and maximum age limits, which exist for safety
and insurance reasons. Some adventure and water activities also have height,
weight, or fitness requirements, or ask participants to declare medical
conditions such as heart problems, pregnancy, or recent surgery. Tell the
operator about allergies before food and drink activities. When unsure whether
an activity is suitable, ask the operator before booking.
