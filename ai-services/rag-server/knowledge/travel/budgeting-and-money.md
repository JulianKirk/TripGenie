# Travel budgeting and money

General guidance for estimating and managing a trip budget. Prices vary by
season and change over time; use these as planning principles rather than
quotes.

## Trip budget: main cost categories

A trip budget usually has five parts: getting there (flights or long-distance
transport), accommodation, local transport, activities and tours, and food and
drink. Accommodation is often the largest single cost after flights. Add a
contingency of about 10 to 15 percent for unplanned costs such as taxis,
baggage fees, and price changes. Travel insurance, visas, and luggage fees are
easy to forget.

## Trip budget: estimating per person and per group

Estimate costs per person per day for food, activities, and local transport,
then multiply by the number of travellers and days. Accommodation is usually
priced per room or per property per night, so sharing a room or an apartment
lowers the cost per person for groups and families. Check whether a tour or
ticket price is per person, per group, or per vehicle before comparing options.

## Trip budget: ways to save

- Travel in the shoulder seasons (autumn and spring) rather than peak holidays.
- Book flights and popular accommodation early, and compare flexible dates.
- Use public transport and multi-day transit passes instead of taxis.
- Choose accommodation with a kitchen and eat some meals in.
- Eat at markets, food courts, and hawker centres.
- Mix paid attractions with free activities such as beaches, parks, markets,
  coastal walks, and free museums.
- Look for combination tickets and multi-attraction passes when visiting
  several paid sights.

## Currencies and payment

- Australia uses the Australian dollar (AUD). Card and contactless payments
  are accepted almost everywhere; some businesses add a card surcharge.
- New Zealand uses the New Zealand dollar (NZD). Cards are widely accepted.
- Japan uses the yen (JPY). Cards are increasingly accepted, but cash is still
  needed at some small restaurants, shrines, and rural businesses; convenience
  store ATMs usually accept foreign cards.
- Singapore uses the Singapore dollar (SGD). Cards are widely accepted; some
  hawker stalls accept only cash or local payment apps.

Tell your bank before travelling and check foreign transaction fees.

## Taxes and tourist refunds

Australia's goods and services tax (GST) is 10 percent and is included in
displayed prices; departing travellers may claim some GST back on eligible
purchases through the Tourist Refund Scheme. New Zealand's GST is 15 percent,
included in displayed prices. Japan's consumption tax is 10 percent, and
eligible foreign visitors can shop tax-free at participating stores with their
passport. Singapore's GST is included in most displayed retail prices, and
tourists can claim refunds on eligible purchases; restaurants often add a 10
percent service charge plus GST to the bill.

## Tipping customs

Tipping is not expected in Australia or New Zealand because service staff are
paid award wages, though rounding up or tipping for exceptional service is
appreciated. In Japan tipping is not customary and can cause confusion. In
Singapore a service charge is often already included in restaurant bills, so
extra tipping is not expected.
