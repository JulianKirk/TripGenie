# Travel logistics and documents

General guidance on documents, flights, time zones, and practical
arrangements. Entry rules change; always confirm requirements with official
government sources for your nationality before booking.

## Travel documents and entry requirements

Check that your passport is valid for at least six months beyond your return
date, as many countries and airlines require this. Visitors to Australia need a
visa or an Electronic Travel Authority (ETA) arranged before travel, except New
Zealand citizens. Most visa-waiver visitors to New Zealand need a New Zealand
Electronic Travel Authority (NZeTA) before travelling, while Australian
citizens do not. Many nationalities, including Australians, can visit Japan
visa-free for short tourist stays. Visitors to Singapore must submit the SG
Arrival Card online before arriving. Requirements depend on nationality, so
confirm them for every traveller in the group.

## Travel insurance and health

Buy travel insurance when you book, so cancellation cover starts early. Check
that the policy covers medical treatment, cancellation, lost luggage, and any
planned adventure activities such as skiing or bungy jumping, which some
policies exclude. Carry prescription medicines in original packaging with a
copy of the prescription. Australia has reciprocal healthcare agreements with
some countries, including New Zealand, but these do not replace insurance.

## Approximate flight times between popular destinations

Direct flight times are approximate and vary by airline, route, and wind:

- Sydney to Melbourne or Brisbane: about 1.5 hours.
- Sydney to Adelaide: about 2 hours.
- Sydney to Hobart: about 2 hours; Melbourne to Hobart: about 1.25 hours.
- Sydney to Perth: about 5 hours westbound (eastbound is usually faster).
- Sydney or Melbourne to Queenstown: about 3 to 3.5 hours.
- Sydney to Singapore: about 8 hours; Perth to Singapore: about 5 hours.
- Sydney to Tokyo: about 9.5 to 10 hours.
- Brisbane to the Gold Coast: about 1 hour by road or train rather than a
  flight.

## Time zones

- Sydney, Melbourne, Hobart, and Brisbane: Australian Eastern Standard Time
  (UTC+10).
- Adelaide: Australian Central Standard Time (UTC+9:30).
- Perth: Australian Western Standard Time (UTC+8).
- Queenstown: New Zealand Standard Time (UTC+12).
- Tokyo: Japan Standard Time (UTC+9), with no daylight saving.
- Singapore: Singapore Standard Time (UTC+8), with no daylight saving.

New South Wales, Victoria, Tasmania, and South Australia use daylight saving
from the first Sunday in October to the first Sunday in April; Queensland and
Western Australia do not. New Zealand uses daylight saving from the last Sunday
in September to the first Sunday in April. Check local times when scheduling
connecting flights and calls.

## Local transport cards and driving

- Sydney: Opal card or contactless bank card.
- Melbourne: myki card or supported contactless payment; free trams in the
  central Free Tram Zone.
- Brisbane and the Gold Coast: go card or contactless payment on Translink
  services.
- Perth: SmartRider or contactless payment on Transperth services.
- Adelaide: metroCARD or contactless payment on Adelaide Metro.
- Tokyo: Suica or PASMO IC cards.
- Singapore: contactless bank cards or stored-value transit cards on the MRT
  and buses.

Australia, New Zealand, Japan, and Singapore all drive on the left. Visitors
driving in Japan need an International Driving Permit issued under the 1949
Geneva Convention, together with their home licence. In Australia and New
Zealand, visitors can usually drive for a limited period on a current overseas
licence in English or with an approved translation. Rental companies often set
minimum driver ages and charge young-driver fees.

## Power plugs and connectivity

Australia and New Zealand use Type I plugs at 230 volts. Japan uses Type A and
B plugs at 100 volts, so check that devices support 100 volts. Singapore uses
Type G plugs at 230 volts. Most phone and laptop chargers support 100 to 240
volts, but hair dryers and other appliances may not. Local SIM cards and eSIMs
are widely available at airports and are usually cheaper than international
roaming.

## Packing checklist

- Passport, visas or travel authorities, and copies stored separately.
- Travel insurance details and emergency contacts.
- Booking confirmations for flights, accommodation, and timed activities.
- Payment cards plus a small amount of local currency.
- Plug adapters and chargers.
- Medicines and prescriptions.
- Clothing layers and rain protection suited to the season.
- Sun protection for Australia and New Zealand.
- Comfortable walking shoes.
