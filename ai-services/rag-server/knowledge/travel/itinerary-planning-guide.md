# Itinerary planning guide

General guidance for turning a list of places and activities into a realistic
trip itinerary.

## Itinerary pacing: how much to plan per day

Plan one or two major activities per day, plus meals and some free time. A
common pattern is one main activity in the morning, a lighter activity in the
afternoon, and a relaxed evening. Packing three or more timed activities into
one day leaves no room for delays, queues, weather, or fatigue. On longer trips
of a week or more, include a lighter rest day roughly every four to five days.

## Itinerary pacing: arrival and departure days

Keep arrival and departure days light. After a long-haul flight, plan only a
short walk, an early dinner, and an easy nearby sight. On departure day, allow
time to check out, store luggage if needed, and reach the airport: about two
hours before domestic flights and three hours before international flights is a
common rule of thumb. For overnight or long-haul arrivals, expect to feel
jet-lagged; getting outside in daylight and adjusting to local meal times helps.

## Itinerary structure: grouping activities by area

Group activities that are close together on the same day to reduce travel time
between them. Check the travel time between each pair of activities, not just
the distance, and add a buffer of 30 to 60 minutes between timed bookings in a
large city. Put activities with fixed start times (tours, shows, restaurant
reservations) into the itinerary first, then fit flexible sightseeing around
them.

## Itinerary structure: day trips and multi-city trips

A day trip works best when the one-way travel time is two hours or less. When
travel is three hours or more each way, an overnight stay is usually better.
For multi-city trips, allow at least two nights in each place; changing
accommodation every night costs time on packing, check-in, and transfers. Treat
a flight between cities as taking about half a day once airport transfers and
check-in are included.

## Itinerary structure: booking ahead

Book ahead for experiences with limited capacity: popular tours, timed-entry
attractions, well-known restaurants, sporting events, festivals, and school
holiday periods. Leave some days less structured so the itinerary can adapt to
weather, energy levels, or a discovery along the way. Check cancellation terms
before paying for non-refundable bookings.

## Planning for families with children

Families travel more slowly: plan fewer activities per day and include a pool,
park, or beach break. Keep travel legs short, schedule demanding activities for
the morning, and check minimum age and height limits for tours and theme-park
rides. Accommodation with a kitchen or separate bedrooms often suits families
better than a single hotel room.

## Planning for groups

For group trips, agree on the budget range, the must-do activities, and the
daily pace before booking. Check that tours and restaurants can take the whole
group, and book group-size tables or tickets early. Build in optional
activities and free time so people with different interests can split up and
regroup.

## Planning for accessibility

Travellers with mobility, sensory, or other access needs should check step-free
access, accessible rooms and bathrooms, lift availability, and accessible
transport before booking. Many major attractions publish accessibility
information and offer companion tickets. Allow extra time between activities
and confirm specific requirements directly with the operator.

## Choosing where to stay

Stay near public transport or within walking distance of the areas you will
visit most. A central location usually costs more per night but saves travel
time and transport costs. For a short city break, one central base is simplest;
for regional travel, match each night's accommodation to the next day's route.
